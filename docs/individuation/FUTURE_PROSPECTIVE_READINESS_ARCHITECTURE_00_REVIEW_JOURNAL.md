### Reviewer A's verdict on the Correction-1 / Correction-2 tension

*(a)* The reading is wrong, and wrong in a specific direction: the licensed half is the **negative** (no
tracking-invalid world, byte-identical replay); the positive phase-trade-off half is contradicted by the same
table. The honest reading of the aggregate record **weakly favours `H_W`, not `H_X`**.

*(b)* **Not a G3 violation.** G3 is textually about parameters, and zero parameters transferred. Part I §11 permits
reading aggregate dispositions, which would be pointless if they could inform nothing. "The problem is not tuning;
it is misreading."

*(c)* Correction 2 undercuts Correction 1 decisively, but in a sharper way than anticipated. The general
objection — mechanism unknown, so no phase reading — is defused, because `H_X` was offered as a hypothesis to be
tested rather than a premise, and Route B's rejection rests on G1/G5 independently. **The fatal version is
narrower: Correction 1 can only survive by invoking Correction 2 to discount the eleven candidates, and the
autopsy names that exact relabelling and forbids it.**

Reviewer A's closing position: Route E **as a design** survives — if the binding constraint was replication density
and IC-class dependence, a properly declared feasibility family over a principled span with independent turnover
evidence is the right response. What does not survive is the framing, the rationale, the promised explanatory
payoff and the terminal ceiling. Under Part I §9 rule 5, **Route F should currently be primary**.

**`REVIEWER_A: FAIL`** — demanding `ARCHITECTURE_REVISE`, explicitly not `STOP_PROSPECTIVE_READINESS`.

### Reviewer B on whether reading the atlas contaminated route selection

*"Yes — demonstrably, and the package says so."* Route E's hypothesis is a direct generalisation of an
atlas-derived reading. **But not fatally**, and the reason is the byte-prefix proof: frozen Part I §11 licenses
reading aggregate dispositions while forbidding world-level influence, and that line was drawn in a commit
verified to have been made **alone, before any route existed**, with zero subsequent deletions. Generating a
hypothesis from exploratory data and testing it prospectively on a fresh, independently-declared family is the
legitimate exploratory-to-confirmatory transition; what matters is that the test region does not inherit the
observation region, which atlas-blind span derivation provides. *"It is not a fig leaf."* The leak is the narrower
B-F2.

### Reviewer B on reproducibility

Given only this architecture, an independent party could **not yet** reproduce a stage-2 family byte-for-byte.
Missing: detector identity; regime-classifier identity; the track-to-world terminal reduction; a definition of
"complete frame pair" and of the declared cadence; and floating-point determinism beyond a dependency lock.

**`REVIEWER_B: FAIL`** — demanding `ARCHITECTURE_REVISE`, explicitly **not** `STOP_PROSPECTIVE_READINESS`:
*"the firewall held, the numeric barrier is exact, the frozen protocol is provably unretrofitted, and the route
ranking survives every attack I could mount … but the frozen protocol states that gates are hard and

## Reviewer independence and limits

Both reviewers ran in separate contexts with their own tool access and were instructed to attack rather than
confirm. Neither could write to any file in the package, and neither could create a commit. Reviewer A had no
device write access; Reviewer B was read-only against the device. Both were explicitly told they could reject, and
both did — including on the point the author had itself flagged as most contestable, and on a point the author had
not anticipated at all.

Two honest limits. First, both reviewers were briefed by the authoring agent, so the framing of what to attack
originated with the author; both nevertheless produced blockers the author had not anticipated — A1–A4 and B-F1.
Second, neither reviewer independently re-derived the frozen scientific baseline; both accepted it as given and
tested conformance to it.

# NEXT SCIENTIFIC ROUTES 01R

## Decision first

No current ETCMNFC target execution is scientifically eligible. The operator is useful, but the joint c/N endpoint
is structurally misspecified for the reported single-body configuration, and the claim that its support is empty at
the **actual exchange-time mask** is not reproducible from the retained allowlisted evidence. The immediate action is
an append-only correction and consolidation, not a target run.

The scores below are **prospective roadmap-priority scores**, not probabilities that a result will be positive. They
use five declared axes: falsifiability/20, identifiability/25, fidelity to the stated question/20, contamination
resistance/15, and information per cost/20. Existing named programmes not audited here remain `NON_SCORÉ`.

## Route comparison

### Route 1 — native core/halo flux on frozen A/B masks

Honest question: does the carrier redistribution change one-step c/N exchange across the frozen dense-component
contour into the **surrounding material**? This is component–surrounding-material exchange, not material–bath flux.

- Intervention: the same predeclared disjoint raw-byte transposition, applied separately from its identity sham.
- Endpoint: signed native face transfers crossing each frozen A/B mask, with every included face having exactly one
  endpoint in that mask; c and N reported separately.
- Engine/LawSpec: no equation change; a new endpoint and new programme identifier are required.
- Exposed data: current DEV geometry and its reported local-interface diagnostics are development-only. No current
  held-out evidence may be used by this reviewer.
- Qualifications: nonempty support before sealing; tap passivity; native-return reconstruction; actual exchange-time
  mask retention; face uniqueness; A/B separation; one-step locality; negative controls for missing, duplicate and
  extra ledger rows; raw arrays retained.
- Dominant contamination risk: the detector mask defines the very contour being credited, and A/B are dense patches
  inside one connected body. The route answers a different question from the original one.
- Failure mode: an apparent differential reflects threshold contour bookkeeping, state-adaptive matching, or a
  common-mode effect cancelled by the antisymmetric contrast.
- Stop: any empty support, treatment-dependent membership, face assigned to both masks, oracle that accepts an extra
  row, or lack of prospective temporal seal.
- Maximum claim: a one-cycle differential in native exchange across frozen component contours under one operator and
  one development-qualified geometry; no body–bath, individuality or identity claim.
- Cost: low to medium.
- **Priority score: 74/100** (18 + 18 + 10 + 12 + 16).

### Route 2 — global exterior body–bath flux after a prospectively justified propagation horizon

Honest question: does the redistribution change total native exchange across the outer boundary of the connected
body after enough time for a signal to arrive? This is a **new systemic hypothesis** with no A/B attribution.

- Intervention: same redistribution versus identity sham.
- Endpoint: total c and N transfers over all native outer-body faces; no Voronoi or component labels.
- Engine/LawSpec: no equation change if the horizon is fixed; a new estimand and programme are required.
- Exposed data: only the current DEV summaries may inform feasibility. The future time series, horizon-qualified
  ledgers and any fresh split remain unexposed and must be sealed before outcome access.
- Qualifications: horizon derived from a source-independent propagation bound; complete time-resolved ledger; fixed
  baseline body mask or an explicit treatment-dependent-set estimand; global mean-field pathway diagnosed.
- Dominant contamination risk: at longer horizons the shared global `up_ref` mixes both components and the alive set
  may itself become post-treatment. A result cannot be assigned to A or B.
- Failure mode: horizon shopping, post-treatment boundary selection, or a global response misdescribed as component
  transport.
- Stop: no pre-result horizon theorem, treatment-dependent support without a declared estimand, or any component-level
  wording.
- Maximum claim: total connected-body boundary flux changes under the intervention after horizon H.
- Cost: medium.
- **Priority score: 48/100** (15 + 9 + 7 + 6 + 11).

### Route 3 — new founding with two disconnected material bodies, each touching the bath

Honest question: does a carrier redistribution between two separately bounded material bodies change each body's
native c/N exchange with its own bath? **This is the only route that literally preserves the original
component–bath question.**

- Intervention: frozen reciprocal carrier redistribution between predeclared internal zones, with an identity sham.
- Endpoint: native body–bath face transfers for each connected body, defined by the kernel predicate.
- Engine/LawSpec: ideally unchanged equations and LawSpec; founding geometry/world is new and therefore requires a
  new authorization, identifier and completely new split.
- Exposed data: none from a future split. Current geometry and every current block remain development diagnostics.
- Qualifications: structural founding rule fixed without outcome data; at least several genuinely distinct
  geometries; exactly two connected bodies; nonempty boundary support for both; no shared global scalar that destroys
  attribution over the frozen window; raw retention; prospective public or third-party timestamped seal.
- Dominant contamination risk: geometry shopping to manufacture support or effect, plus loss of comparability with
  the original founding distribution.
- Failure mode: bodies merge, one lacks support, the intervention changes viability, or the global mean field creates
  cross-body interference before measurement.
- Stop: any geometry selected from target effects, effective geometry n=1, empty support, or unsealable protocol.
- Maximum claim: differential native body–bath transfer in a newly founded two-body model family; still no ownership,
  individuality or life claim.
- Cost: high.
- **Priority score: 82/100** (19 + 22 + 20 + 13 + 8).

### Route 4 — separate zone/reservoir interventions for contributions to global flux

Question: can interventions on A, B and the reservoir separately identify their contributions to total outer-body
flux despite a shared connected body?

- Intervention: factorial A-only, B-only, A+B and sham perturbations, with fixed doses and no endpoint-conditioned
  matching.
- Endpoint: total outer-body c/N flux plus interaction contrasts; no artificial face ownership.
- Engine/LawSpec: operator schedule changes; equations can remain unchanged.
- Exposed data: current DEV geometry may be used only to design feasibility checks; no arm-specific future response,
  allocation or support may be exposed before the factorial protocol is sealed.
- Qualifications: interference model, positivity of every arm, stable support, common-mode diagnostics and a
  predeclared estimand under interference.
- Dominant contamination risk: a shared mean-field response can be misread as an A- or B-specific contribution.
- Failure mode: shared dynamics violate additivity, so contribution language becomes model-dependent.
- Stop: a component arm changes the support definition or a decomposition requires untested linearity.
- Maximum claim: controlled global-flux contrasts under a stated interference model, not native A/B ownership.
- Cost: high.
- **Priority score: 55/100** (17 + 12 + 9 + 8 + 9).

### Route 5 — passive tracers and temporal provenance

Question: where do carrier-associated material and public-field changes travel, and when, without assigning causal
ownership from position alone?

- Intervention: none for qualification; passive labels only. Later causal interventions remain separate.
- Endpoint: complete tracer conservation/provenance, time-resolved native face crossings and lineage uncertainty.
- Engine/LawSpec: instrumentation/state extension is required and must be proven passive against the scalar reference
  path; this is not the current unchanged model.
- Exposed data: synthetic fixtures and current DEV states only during qualification; future tracer histories and
  intervention-linked lineage must remain behind a new split and access ledger.
- Qualifications: bitwise/no-tolerance passivity where possible, conservation, resolution sensitivity, association
  edge retention and actual exchange-time masks.
- Dominant contamination risk: tracer identity becomes a hidden physics channel.
- Failure mode: a diagnostic ID influences association, conservation fails or ambiguity is forcibly resolved.
- Stop: any public-state difference, insufficient tracer resolution, or forced lineage through ambiguity.
- Maximum claim: descriptive transport/provenance under a qualified passive instrument; not persistent identity.
- Cost: medium to high.
- **Priority score: 64/100** (17 + 17 + 8 + 13 + 9).

### Route 6 — constructed partition of the external boundary

Question: under an explicitly artificial Voronoi/geodesic partition, how is total body–bath flux allocated to A and
B accounting regions?

- Intervention: unchanged carrier redistribution versus identity sham.
- Endpoint: outer faces assigned to A/B accounting regions by the constructed rule, reported alongside the uncut
  global total.
- Engine/LawSpec: no equation change; the observer ontology changes materially.
- Exposed data: the accounting rule may be stress-tested on synthetic and current DEV geometry only; no future
  target effect may be inspected while selecting distances, ties or partition parameters.
- Qualification: partition invariance/sensitivity, ties, periodic geometry, must-fail one-body aliases.
- Dominant contamination risk: false precision; the partition creates ownership not present in the native model.
- Failure mode: conclusions change materially with distance, tie or periodic-wrap convention.
- Stop: any wording that calls the partition native or causal.
- Maximum claim: flux under one accounting convention only.
- Cost: low.
- **Priority score: 28/100** (12 + 5 + 3 + 3 + 5).

### Route 7 — modify the native `alive` predicate

Question: does another material threshold produce a boundary closer to A/B?

- Intervention: the same carrier redistribution versus identity sham, but only after defining a new observer/model
  family prospectively; no threshold may be chosen by looking at its target contrast.
- Endpoint: threshold-relative native face transfers and connectivity, explicitly scoped to the new predicate.
- Engine/LawSpec: this changes the measurement ontology and may change dynamics if the predicate is used by the
  kernel. It is a new model/observer family.
- Exposed data: synthetic threshold fixtures and the already exposed DEV topology only; prospective responses and
  every held-out threshold outcome remain sealed.
- Qualification: threshold fixed from non-outcome criteria, scalar-reference parity if kernel behavior changes,
  topology/support stability, sensitivity grid declared in advance and a new temporal seal.
- Dominant contamination risk: direct threshold tuning to obtain nonempty support.
- Failure mode: at the dense-component threshold the reported body fragments, while at the kernel threshold A/B sit
  in one body; the endpoint therefore changes discontinuously with observer choice.
- Stop: any threshold chosen after inspecting target effects or called a correction to the old endpoint.
- Maximum claim: threshold-relative geometry in a new declared model, never a rescue of ETCMNFC.
- Cost: medium.
- **Priority score: 14/100** (8 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 2).

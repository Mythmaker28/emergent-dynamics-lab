\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The extent of a field maintained by a localised source in a discrete, capacity-limited medium is
set jointly by transport, mortality and the source's own history, and a continuum reading of it
is not available at the scale studied here: it errs by \absContinuumErrorStaticPercent\pc{} and
\absContinuumErrorMobilePercent\pc{} against the discrete operator. Such extents are ordinarily
reported after the fact, so it is rarely possible to say whether a model predicted them or
accommodated them. We therefore constructed a one-step conditional operator directly from frozen
kinetics, derived an equivalence margin of \equivalenceMarginPercent\pc{} from named error terms,
froze two absolute predictions and their ratio, hashed the analysis code, and only then ran
\freshArmsRun{} fresh arms. The observed medians met the frozen predictions to
$\freshStaticDeviationPercent\pc$ and $+\freshMobileDeviationPercent\pc$, and the mobility ratio
to $+\freshRatioDeviationPercent\pc$ with unity excluded; every interval lay wholly inside the
margin, with no extinction and no invalid arm. We then asked why an earlier record had shown
deficits of $\cellStaticMedianObservedPercent\pc$ and $\cellMobileMedianObservedPercent\pc$. Over
\radialProfileMobileArms{} historical arms the cumulative radial profile follows the operator at
every one of \radialProfileRadiiTested{} radii, maximum absolute $z$ of
\radialProfileMaxAbsZ{}, so the field was never in deficit; a surrogate with no lattice dynamics
at all already carries $\cellStaticMedianSurrogatePercent\pc$ to
$\cellMobileMedianSurrogatePercent\pc$, and the remainder requires the source's own wandering,
which moves the construction from $\mSixSequentialStepZeroBaselineMTwoLevel\pc$ to
$\mSixSequentialStepOneAddSharedTrajectory\pc$ and then to
$\mSixSequentialStepTwoAddEmpiricalBirthFlux\pc$ once the empirical birth flux is added. The
operator is exact given the state but the marginal density equation does not close, and the
qualification holds at one parameter point.
\end{abstract}

\section{The quantity, and why it is not trivial}

A localised source injects particles into a medium; the particles move, and they die. At
stationarity the field around the source has a characteristic extent. Writing down that extent
looks like a textbook exercise: balance injection against decay, take the diffusion limit, read
off a localisation length. The exercise fails here for three separate reasons, and the failures
are the reason the quantity is worth measuring rather than deriving.

First, the medium is discrete and the reported extent is quantised. The observable used
throughout this paper, written $r_{80}$, is the smallest \emph{achievable} lattice distance at
which the empirical cumulative field first reaches $0.8$. It is therefore supported on the
discrete set of achievable toroidal distances, not on the real line, and near the values of
interest that set is coarse. A continuum expression with the same localisation length is not a
good approximation to it: against the discrete operator it errs by
$\continuumErrorStaticPercent\pc$ in the static condition and $\continuumErrorMobilePercent\pc$
in the mobile one. Coarse-graining the lattice away is not available at this scale
\citep{hughes1995,spitzer1976}.

Second, transport is capacity-limited. A move is refused when the destination cell is full, so
the one-step transport factor is a function of the occupancy, which is itself the quantity being
propagated. The equation for the mean density therefore does not close; we return to this in
Section~\ref{sec:construction} and again in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}, because it is the
principal thing this paper does \emph{not} resolve \citep{liggett1985}.

Third, the source has a history. When the source is mobile, every particle alive at a given
instant was born somewhere along one shared trajectory. Their displacements are not independent
draws around a fixed point: they share a common, autocorrelated origin process. Ignoring that
shared history is the single largest error one can make in this system, as
Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} shows quantitatively.

Because of these three features, the extent is a measured quantity in a model, and the
methodological question becomes sharp: can it be \emph{predicted}? Extents of this kind are
ordinarily reported after the data exist, which makes it impossible to distinguish a model that
predicted a number from one that accommodated it. The design below was built to remove that
ambiguity, at the cost of committing publicly to two numbers and a tolerance before running
anything.

\section{Model and observable}
\label{sec:model}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig1_model_and_event_order.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The model and the reported observable.} \textbf{(a)}~A point source on a
periodic lattice with a per-cell capacity; the reported radius is the smallest achievable
toroidal distance at which the empirical cumulative field first reaches $0.8$. The particle
positions drawn here are schematic and are not measured data. \textbf{(b)}~The event order,
read directly from the kinetics module rather than from any description of it.}
\label{fig:model}
\end{figure}

The medium is a periodic square lattice of side $L=36$ with a capacity of $16$ particles per
cell. A single source cell injects particles of the reported species; particles hop to nearest
neighbours with probability $p_{\mathrm{hop}}=\pHop$ per step, subject to capacity, and die with
probability $\muX$ per step. Two conditions are compared: \emph{static}, in which the source
is immobilised, and \emph{mobile}, in which it performs its own walk. Nothing else differs
between them.

The event order within one step matters and was read from the kinetics module itself, not from a
description of it: transport of each species in turn, then reaction, then decay, then feed and
outflow (Figure~\ref{fig:model}b). One consequence is used later: because reaction runs after
transport, a particle born in step $t$ takes no step in step~$t$. Every one of the
\birthPositionTotalBirthRecords{} birth records examined across \birthPositionArms{} arms occurred
at the source cell exactly, with maximum coordinate offsets of
$\birthPositionMaxAbsDy{}$ and $\birthPositionMaxAbsDx{}$; the point-source idealisation is not an
assumption here but a checked property of the implementation.

Each run of the model is called an \emph{arm} and is the unit of analysis throughout. An arm runs
for $11\,000$ steps, of which the first $2\,000$ are discarded as burn-in, and the field is
sampled every $50$ steps thereafter, giving $180$ frames per arm. The within-arm summary is the
\emph{median} of $r_{80}$ over those frames; that rule was fixed by an earlier protocol and is
inherited here unchanged, which turns out to matter a great deal
(Section~\ref{sec:estimator}). Across arms we take the arithmetic mean. Frames, steps, particles
and birth events are never treated as independent replicates for any confirmatory statement.

The burn-in is adequate for the mass but only marginally so for the shape. The mass relaxes with
an e-folding time of \relaxationMassEFolding{} steps, so the burn-in is
\relaxationBurnInInMassEFoldings{} e-foldings and leaves a residual mass deficit of
\relaxationResidualMassDeficitAtTheStartOfTheWindow{}. The slowest shape mode on this torus has a
time constant of \relaxationSlowestTorusShapeMode{} steps, so the same burn-in is only
\relaxationBurnInInShapeEFoldings{} e-foldings of it and leaves a residual shape deficit of
\relaxationResidualShapeDeficitAtTheStartOfTheWindow{}. That finite-time effect is not assumed
away: it is carried inside the construction, which starts from an empty lattice and burns in for
exactly the protocol duration.

\section{Construction of the operator, and the freeze}
\label{sec:construction}

\subsection{The one-step conditional operator}

Conditional on the state $S_t$, the expected occupancy vector obeys
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[n(t+1) \,\middle|\, S_t\right] \;=\; T(S_t)\, n(t) \;+\; b(S_t),
\label{eq:operator}
\end{equation}
and this form is exact for this engine, not an approximation to it. Transport contributes a
$T$ that is a function of the occupancy, because the number of movers accepted out of a cell is
the minimum of the number offered and the free capacity at the destination; reaction contributes
a $b$ that is likewise state-dependent, being the minimum of the available substrate and the free
capacity at the source cell; and decay contributes an exactly linear factor $(1-\muX)$ with no
state dependence at all. In the surviving record, births equalled that minimum in
\couplingFractionOfStepsWhereBirthsEqualMinnsxfree{} of steps, and the source cell had zero free
capacity in \couplingFractionOfStepsWithZeroFreeCapacityAtTheOrganiser{} of them, measured over
\couplingArms{} arms.

Equation~\eqref{eq:operator} does not survive taking expectations. Since $T$ and $n$ are both
functions of the same occupancy, $\mathbb{E}[T(S)n] \neq \mathbb{E}[T(S)]\,\mathbb{E}[n]$, and the
marginal density equation therefore does not close \citep{vankampen2007,gardiner2009}. What
\emph{is} available is that the unblocked limit is a genuine closed linear operator, and that the
departure from it is bounded: over a mean lifetime a particle is offered
\capacityPerLifetimeOfferedHops{} hops and expects \capacityPerLifetimeExpectedRefusals{}
refusals, so \capacityCertifiedFractionNeverRefused{} of particles are never refused at all.
Removing that fraction of moves, and concentrating every removal adversarially on outward moves,
changes the reported radius by \capacityImpliedChangeInREightZeroPercent\pc{}. The stationary
profile therefore closes approximately, with a certified bound; the marginal density does not
close at all. We record both, and we do not present the first as though it were the second.

\subsection{Terms measured, and terms dropped}

Four candidate corrections were measured before the confirmation arms were designed, and three
of them were dropped on the evidence rather than on judgement. The finite torus contributes
nothing to the reported radius, because that radius is quantised to achievable lattice distances
whose spacing near these values exceeds the correction. The finite horizon contributes
$\relaxationBurnInInMassEFoldings$ e-foldings of mass relaxation and is carried inside the
construction rather than corrected for. The intra-step order contributes
\marginIntraStepOrderResidualPercent\pc{}. Capacity rejection contributes
\marginCapacityCertifiedErrorOnREightZeroPercent\pc{}. Two terms were retained because they are
large: the shared source trajectory, and the endogeneity of the birth flux. The birth flux is
over-dispersed relative to Poisson, with a variance-to-mean ratio of
\birthFluxVarianceOverMean{}, and the stationary population it implies,
\populationCheckNXStarPredictedAsBOverMu{}, agrees with the observed
\populationCheckNXObserved{} to a ratio of \populationCheckRatio{}. The age distribution of the
standing population is the geometric one \citep{feller1968} to within a ratio of \ageRatio{}
across \ageMolecules{} particles.

\subsection{The equivalence margin, and what it is not}

The tolerance was derived before any confirmation arm existed, from named terms, and was not
chosen to accommodate anything. The model-error terms combine in quadrature to
\marginModelErrorQuadraturePercent\pc{}; two sampling standard errors at \budgetArmsPerCondition{}
arms per condition amount to \marginTwoSamplingSePercent\pc{}; the declared margin is
\equivalenceMarginPercent\pc{}. The point that matters is the referent \citep{wellek2010}: the margin is applied to
the distance between the observation and the construction's \emph{point prediction}, not to the
distance from zero. A margin built to contain the historical numbers instead would have had to be
about twice as wide.

\subsection{Freeze and inertness}

Before any confirmation arm ran, the two absolute predictions and their ratio were written into a
protocol file and the analysis modules were hashed; the frozen predictions carry the status
\texttt{\predictionStatus{}} and the methods hash \texttt{\methodsCoreHashStatus{}}. The
apparatus that records the runs was checked separately: over \inertnessSteps{} steps an
instrumented engine and a plain one reach a bit-identical state, hash
\texttt{\inertnessStateHash{}}, so
\texttt{INSTRUMENTATION\_\allowbreak{}ALTERS\_\allowbreak{}THE\_\allowbreak{}LAW}~\texttt{=}~\texttt{\inertnessAltersTheLaw}. This is one
paired run at one seed; it falsifies interference, it does not prove inertness for every
trajectory.

\section{The residual was in the summary rule, not in the field}
\label{sec:estimator}

\label{fig:estimator}
\end{figure}

The construction just tested was not built in a vacuum. An earlier record, made under the same
frozen rules, had shown the reported radius falling \emph{below} the ideal operator's value:
$\cellStaticMedianObservedPercent\pc$ in the static condition and
$\cellMobileMedianObservedPercent\pc$ in the mobile one, the latter over
\historicalMobileMedianResidualN{} arms at $z=\historicalMobileMedianResidualZ{}$. A deficit that
size, that consistent, and that clearly larger in the mobile condition reads like physics. It is
not.

\subsection{The field agrees at every radius}

The first thing to check is whether the shortfall is in the field or in how the field is
summarised. Over \radialProfileMobileArms{} historical mobile arms the observed cumulative radial
profile follows the operator at every one of \radialProfileRadiiTested{} radii: the largest
standardised deviation is \radialProfileMaxAbsZ{} and the largest difference in cumulative
probability is \radialProfileMaxAbsDifference{} (Figure~\ref{fig:estimator}a). The cloud is where
the operator says it is. Only the summarised quantile is not.

The same comparison in the static condition is \emph{not} informative and we do not use it. It
rests on \radialProfileStaticArms{} arms. Its largest standardised deviation is
\radialProfileStaticMaxAbsZ{}, but that occurs at a radius where the absolute difference,
\radialProfileStaticMaxAbsDifference{}, is no larger than at radii whose standardised deviation is
below one --- a standard error estimated from three arms can collapse by chance, and here it did.
The flag recorded in the source programme is scoped
\texttt{\radialProfileFlagScope{}} accordingly, and we report the static cell as not evaluated
rather than quietly averaging it in.

\subsection{A surrogate with no dynamics reproduces part of the deficit}

Take the operator's own stationary law, draw particles from it independently at the observed
per-frame counts, and apply the identical summary rule. There is no lattice, no transport, no
capacity, no source: only the estimator. That surrogate is already biased downwards, by
$\cellStaticMedianSurrogatePercent\pc$ in the static condition and
$\cellMobileMedianSurrogatePercent\pc$ in the mobile one, over \iidStaticArms{} surrogate arms of
\iidStaticFrames{} frames each. The mechanism is elementary and was flagged in the estimator's own
definition: $r_{80}$ is a finite-sample quantile of a first crossing, which is a minimum over
distance, and minima of noisy crossings are biased downwards
\citep{serfling1980,redner2001}.

That accounts for most of the static deficit --- $\cellStaticMedianSurrogatePercent\pc$ against
$\cellStaticMedianObservedPercent\pc$ observed --- and we say so no more strongly than that.

\subsection{It does not account for the mobile deficit}

In the mobile condition the surrogate reaches only
$\cellMobileMedianSurrogatePercent\pc$ against $\cellMobileMedianObservedPercent\pc$ observed
(Figure~\ref{fig:estimator}c). Roughly three quarters of the mobile deficit is not an estimator
artefact in this sense, and something in the dynamics has to supply it. What supplies it is the
source's own wandering, quantified in Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}: adding the shared trajectory
moves the construction from $\mSixSequentialStepZeroBaselineMTwoLevel\pc$ to
$\mSixSequentialStepOneAddSharedTrajectory\pc$, and adding the empirical birth flux takes it to
$\mSixSequentialStepTwoAddEmpiricalBirthFlux\pc$, against
$\cellMobileMedianObservedPercent\pc$ observed. The construction slightly overshoots; its own
Monte-Carlo standard error at that point is \marginMSixMonteCarloSeMobilePercent\pc{}.

\subsection{Dispersion orders the two accounts}

Dispersion separates the accounts more cleanly than the central value does. The observed
within-arm standard deviation in the mobile condition is \dispersionMobileWithinArmSd{}; the
surrogate produces \dispersionIidMobileWithinArmSd{}, less than half of it; the full construction
produces \mSixDispersionMobileWithinArmSdSimulated{}
(Figure~\ref{fig:estimator}d). On the fresh arms the same comparison was a pre-declared secondary
control and was met: predicted \secondaryWithinArmSdMobilePredicted{} against
\secondaryWithinArmSdMobileObserved{} observed in the mobile condition, and
\secondaryWithinArmSdStaticPredicted{} against \secondaryWithinArmSdStaticObserved{} in the
static. The mobile per-frame series is also right-skewed, at
\dispersionMobileWithinArmSkew{} against \dispersionStaticWithinArmSkew{} in the static
condition, which is what puts a median below its mean.

\subsection{Changing the summary rule removes most of the discrepancy}

On identical frames, the historical mobile deficit is
$\cellMobileMedianObservedPercent\pc$ under the frozen median rule and
$\cellMobileMeanObservedPercent\pc$ under the mean rule (Figure~\ref{fig:estimator}b). The choice
of summary rule, not the field, carries the bulk of it. The mean rule was also a pre-declared
control on the fresh arms and was met:
$\secondaryMeanSummaryStaticObservedPercent\pc$ observed against
$\secondaryMeanSummaryStaticPredicted\pc$ predicted in the static condition, and
$\secondaryMeanSummaryMobileObservedPercent\pc$ against
$\secondaryMeanSummaryMobilePredicted\pc$ in the mobile one.

Two qualifications belong here rather than in the discussion. The mean rule is not unbiased ---
under the surrogate it is in fact slightly \emph{worse} than the median in the static condition,
$\cellStaticMeanSurrogatePercent\pc$ against $\cellStaticMedianSurrogatePercent\pc$ --- it is
merely less biased where it matters. And the construction's largest single disagreement with the
historical record is here: it predicts $\cellMobileMeanConstructionPercent\pc$ for the mobile mean
against $\cellMobileMeanObservedPercent\pc$ observed, an overshoot of about a percentage point
and a quarter. That cell is the weakest agreement in this paper and we do not present it as
though it were among the strongest.

\section{What the agreement depends on}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig4_mechanism_ablation.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{What the agreement depends on.} \textbf{(a)}~Distance between the observed
mobile radius and four constructions. \textbf{(b)}~The same constructions built up in physical
order. \textbf{(c)}~The two main effects and their interaction in a two-by-two decomposition.}
\label{fig:mechanism}
\end{figure}

An agreement is only informative if one can say what it would have taken to break it. We
therefore removed each retained ingredient in turn and measured how far the prediction moved from
the observed mobile radius, \ablationObservedMobileMedian{}. The full construction sits
\ablationDistFull{} lattice units away. Replacing the endogenous birth flux by a Poisson source of
the same mean moves it to \ablationDistPoisson{}, about four and a half times further. Removing
the shared source trajectory moves it to \ablationDistNoShared{}, about fifteen times further.
The uncorrected ideal value, \ablationPredIdeal{}, is furthest of all
(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}a).

Built up in physical order rather than torn down (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}b), the discrete
kernel with a Poisson source gives $\mSixSequentialStepZeroBaselineMTwoLevel\pc$; adding the
shared trajectory gains $\mSixSequentialStepOneGain{}$ percentage points; adding the empirical
birth flux gains a further $\mSixSequentialStepTwoGain{}$. In a two-by-two design over the same
two factors the main effects are $\mSixFactorialMainEffectSharedTrajectory{}$ and
$\mSixFactorialMainEffectBirthFlux{}$ percentage points and the interaction is
$+\mSixFactorialInteraction{}$, which is small enough that the two ingredients can be read
additively here \citep{boxhunterhunter2005,cox1958}. We note the standing caveat on such
decompositions: the two main effects and the interaction sum to the total by construction, so
their sum is not independent evidence for either.

The reason the shared trajectory matters so much is visible in the dispersion. Removing it
collapses the within-arm standard deviation from \mSixDispersionMobileWithinArmSdSimulated{} to
\ablationNoSharedTrajectoryWithinArmSd{} --- that is, onto the static width --- and the
median penalty collapses with it. The mobile deficit is not a different physical regime; it is
the same estimator acting on a wider per-frame distribution, and the distribution is wider
because every particle alive at a given moment was born somewhere along one wandering path.

Two ingredients that might have been expected to matter do not. Capacity is one: blocking is
\blockedFractionXMean{} on average and its adversarial effect on the reported radius is
\capacityImpliedChangeInREightZeroPercent\pc{}, two orders of magnitude below the residual under
study. The finite torus is the other: it leaves the reported radius unchanged at every size
tested, because that radius is quantised more coarsely than the correction.

\section{Project history, and what did not survive}
\label{sec:history}

This paper is one result of a longer programme, and honesty about the rest of that programme is
part of reporting this one.

A line of work addressed to a different question --- whether a region of the parameter space of
this architecture has certain properties --- was carried out in earlier sessions. It included a
prospective calibration exercise and a subsequent arbitration over which route to take. The raw
archives of that work did not survive the container resets during which this paper was written.
Their narrative records exist outside this repository; their bytes do not. No quantity, figure,
table or abstract sentence in this paper draws on any of it, and none of it is reconstructed from
memory here.

What follows from that, stated plainly: whether the architecture admits a parameter region with
the properties that line of work was asking about is \textbf{not tested} by this paper. The
status is \texttt{NOT\_\allowbreak{}TESTED}, which is a statement about the absence of a test
and not about the answer. A reader will find no sentence anywhere in this paper asserting a
negative answer to that question either; nothing here bears on it in either direction, and the
evidence that might have borne on it is no longer available to be examined.

The historical arms used in Section~\ref{sec:estimator} come from programmes whose raw archives
are likewise not all present in this session. They are used descriptively --- to characterise a
record that motivated the analysis --- and never as confirmatory replicates. Every confirmatory
statement in this paper rests on the \freshArmsRun{} fresh arms of
Section~\ref{sec:confirmation}, whose seeds, final state hashes and ledger sizes are listed in
the supplement.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Three things are established here, at one parameter point.

An operator built from the microscopic rules, with two named corrections and a margin derived
from named error terms, predicted the stationary extent of a source-maintained field before the
confirming data existed, and the data met it. The agreement is not confined to the summary
statistic; the full radial profile follows the operator over \radialProfileMobileArms{} arms.
Mobility increases the extent, by an amount the construction anticipated to within
$+\freshRatioDeviationPercent\pc$.

A residual that had every appearance of physics was largely an artefact of a summary rule. That
diagnosis is worth more than the specific number attached to it, because the ingredients are
generic: a first-crossing quantile is a minimum, minima of noisy crossings are biased downwards,
right-skewed per-frame distributions put medians below means, and a wandering source widens the
per-frame distribution without moving the underlying law. Any measurement that summarises a
first-crossing length within a replicate and then averages across replicates is exposed to the
same effect.

The agreement depends on ingredients that can be named and removed, and removing them degrades
it by factors of four and fifteen. That is what distinguishes this from a fit.

What is not established is at least as important. The marginal density equation does not close,
and nothing here closes it; the stationary profile closes only approximately, with a certified
bound, and the reader should not read the agreement in Section~\ref{sec:confirmation} as an
agreement of a closed theory. The qualification holds at one lattice size, one source strength
and two mobility settings; the dependence on hop rate is a contrast, not a curve. The

percentage point and a quarter. And the static radial profile comparison, on three arms, is not
